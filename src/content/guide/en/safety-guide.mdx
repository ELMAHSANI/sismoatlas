## Before: get prepared

- In each room, identify a sturdy shelter (solid table or desk) and places to avoid: windows, mirrors, tall furniture.
- Secure tall furniture and heavy objects to the wall; store heavy items low down.
- Know how to turn off gas, water and electricity.
- Prepare an emergency bag: water, non-perishable food, torch, batteries, first-aid kit, medicines, copies of important documents, phone charger.
- Agree on an outdoor meeting point with your family.
- Build or renovate according to the seismic building code (see the article on RPS 2000).

## During: protect yourself

<ThreeActions />

**Indoors: Drop, Cover and Hold On.**

- Drop to your knees, take cover under a sturdy table and hold on to its leg until the shaking stops.
- No table: get next to an interior wall and protect your head and neck with your arms.
- Do not run for the exit or use stairs or lifts while the ground is shaking. Stay away from windows.

**Outdoors**: move away from buildings, walls, poles and power lines, drop and protect your head.

**In a car**: stop away from bridges, tunnels and buildings, and stay in the vehicle with your seatbelt on.

**By the sea**: if the shaking is strong or long, move away from the coast to high ground immediately, without waiting for a warning (tsunami risk).

## After: act safely

- Expect aftershocks: drop, cover and hold on again each time.
- Leave carefully if the building is damaged; do not use the lift.
- If you smell gas: no flames or light switches, open the windows, turn off the gas and get out.
- Help people nearby without putting yourself at risk; do not move a seriously injured person.
- Listen to the radio and follow the authorities' instructions; only use the phone for emergencies.
- Do not re-enter a cracked building until it has been inspected.
- Beware of rumours: no earthquake "prediction" is reliable.

## Study area

The "Morocco and surroundings" area spans latitudes **20° N to 38° N** and longitudes **18° W to 1° E**: the whole of Morocco (southern provinces included), the Alboran Sea, southern Spain and Portugal, and western Algeria.

## Two complementary catalogues

**1. Reference catalogue, 1045–2005** — Peláez et al. (2007), 1,738 earthquakes. It compiles historical and instrumental earthquakes in the region (27° N–37° N, 15° W–1° E) from the catalogues of IGN (Spain), the Service de Physique du Globe in Rabat, CRAAG (Algeria), ISC, NEIC and published studies. Its magnitudes have been **unified as moment magnitude (Mw)** using empirical relationships. It is **declustered**: foreshocks and aftershocks were removed with the Gardner and Knopoff (1974) method, then only events with Mw ≥ 3.0 were kept.

**2. Seismicity since 2006** — online catalogues of the EMSC (which relays, among others, the Spanish IGN and Morocco's CNRST) and the USGS. It contains **all** detected earthquakes, aftershocks included, with different, unconverted magnitude types (ml, mb, mbLg, Mw…).

The two datasets **are not directly comparable**: they are shown as separate layers and statistics are computed only within the same dataset.

## Updates

- **Recent earthquakes (Morocco, 30 days) and major earthquakes worldwide (M ≥ 6, 1 year)**: downloaded automatically every 15 minutes; the page also receives new earthquakes live (EMSC feed).
- **Seismicity since 2006**: re-downloaded periodically (agencies revise their solutions).
- **Reference catalogue**: fixed (2007 publication).

## Merging and de-duplication

The same earthquake is often published by several agencies. Two records are considered **the same earthquake** if their times differ by **20 seconds at most** and their epicentres by **50 km at most**. For the Morocco area, the **EMSC is the reference agency** (it relays the regional agencies); the other agency's identifier (USGS) is kept. The reference catalogue and recent seismicity cover different periods (up to 2005 / since 2006) and are not merged.

## Plain-language descriptions

- **Nearest city**: computed from the GeoNames list (places with 5,000 inhabitants or more; secondary landmark: cities of 100,000 or more). The direction is given from the city to the epicentre. It is approximate: the epicentre itself is uncertain.
- **Depth**: shallow (less than 70 km), intermediate (70 to 300 km), deep (more than 300 km), following the usual classes.
- **Time**: agencies publish in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). Morocco time is computed by your browser with the official `Africa/Casablanca` time zone (Morocco returned to GMT on 20 September 2026).

## "Earthquakes in Morocco" section

By default, only earthquakes of magnitude **2.5 or more** are shown ("Show all earthquakes" button). The **Canary Islands** are not shown in this section: their activity, often volcanic (La Palma swarm in 2021), is not felt in Morocco; they remain visible on the full map.

## Major earthquakes worldwide

Earthquakes of magnitude 6 or more published by the USGS. The **PAGER level** is an automatic, revisable estimate of likely human and economic losses. The **tsunami flag** marks a strong offshore earthquake for which warning centres may issue information: it does not mean a tsunami occurred.

## Data limitations

- The number of small earthquakes detected rises as station networks become denser: more earthquakes does not necessarily mean more activity.
- In seismicity since 2006, the annual number of earthquakes also depends on how the EMSC aggregates small events from agencies (a large apparent drop between 2012 and 2019 in our data).
- Old locations are imprecise (sometimes by tens of kilometres); some depths are fixed by default (0, 10 or 33 km).
- Recent magnitudes of different types are not all equivalent to Mw.
- Older aftershock sequences (before the 2000s) are incomplete in online catalogues.
- SismoAtlas makes **no forecasts** and does not replace the authorities.
